## arxiv astro-ph

### The Key to Unlocking Exoplanet Biosignatures: a UK-led IR Spectrograph for the Habitable Worlds Observatory Coronagraph
- **Authors:** UK-led collaboration (March 2026)
- **Summary:** This white paper proposes a UK-led near-infrared spectrograph for the Habitable Worlds Observatory's coronagraph. The paper argues that there is no single spectral feature that constitutes an unambiguous biosignature. Instead, the strategy should focus on detecting multiple molecular features that shouldn't coexist in chemical equilibrium, such as O₂ alongside CH₄, across a broad wavelength range.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09321

### Exoplanet Characterization with NASA's Habitable Worlds Observatory
- **Authors:** NASA HWO collaboration (January 2026)
- **Summary:** A comprehensive review of exoplanet characterization capabilities for NASA's Habitable Worlds Observatory, planned for launch in the mid-2040s. The mission is expected to detect biosignature molecules and habitability indicators including vegetation red edge on potentially habitable worlds.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06233

### JWST Unveils High Mean Molecular Weight Atmosphere for Mini-Neptune TOI-1130b
- **Authors:** Exoplanet research team (May 2026)
- **Summary:** JWST transmission spectroscopy reveals that TOI-1130b, a warm mini-Neptune with a radius of 3.66 Earth radii and equilibrium temperature near 825 K, possesses a volatile-rich atmosphere. The system also hosts an inner mini-Neptune and outer hot Jupiter in a 2:1 mean motion resonance. The volatile-rich composition and orbital configuration support formation beyond the water ice line followed by planetary migration.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02036

## phys.org

### Atmosphere Detected on Habitable-Zone Rocky World
- **Summary:** Astronomers detected helium escaping from LHS 1140 b, a rocky planet orbiting in its star's habitable zone about 48 light-years away (July 2026). This provides the first clear evidence of a long-lived atmosphere on a terrestrial-type exoplanet in a habitable zone. Researchers plan to determine the atmosphere's full composition and search for surface oceans.
- **Link:** https://phys.org/news/2026-07-life-solar-atmosphere-habitable-zone.html

### Study Finds TRAPPIST-1e Unlikely to Have Venus- or Mars-like Atmosphere
- **Summary:** Recent JWST observations of TRAPPIST-1e suggest the planet may still possess an atmosphere, but a thick, hydrogen-rich one similar to Venus or Mars appears unlikely (September 2025). This finding refines our understanding of atmospheric retention on terrestrial exoplanets in M-dwarf systems.
- **Link:** https://phys.org/news/2025-09-exoplanet-trappist-1e-venus-mars.html

## Speculative Corner

The recent detection of an atmosphere around LHS 1140 b marks a pivotal moment in the search for extraterrestrial life. A rocky exoplanet with a long-lived atmosphere represents precisely the kind of world where microbial life could persist. The key question now becomes: what is that atmosphere composed of?

If LHS 1140 b's atmosphere contains significant amounts of oxygen, carbon dioxide, and methane, we might be looking at a world where photosynthetic or chemosynthetic microbial life could thrive. Earth's early biosphere—dominated by anaerobic microbes for nearly 2 billion years—demonstrates that life can flourish without oxygen. However, if we detect oxygen, it would suggest biological activity similar to Earth's—a powerful biosignature.

The JWST observations of mini-Neptunes like TOI-1130b reveal that planets formed beyond the water ice line can retain volatile-rich atmospheres and potentially aqueous environments. This expands the habitable zone beyond traditional estimates. A warm mini-Neptune with substantial water and organic compounds in its atmosphere represents another tantalizing target for harboring simple microbial life adapted to higher pressures and different atmospheric compositions than Earth's microbes. Such organisms might rely on chemosynthesis around hydrothermal vents on the ocean floor rather than photosynthesis, making them harder to detect but equally plausible.
