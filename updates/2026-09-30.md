# Exosphere Newsletter — 2026-09-30

## Highlights

**Sub-Neptune Population Characterization**: A new study of the young two-planet system HIP 67522 bc provides crucial transit timing variation masses for understanding super-Earths and sub-Neptunes, confirming predictions from contracting atmosphere planet formation models. This work advances our understanding of the most common planetary types in the galaxy.

**Water Ice Line Discoveries**: The detection of TOI-6981 b, a transiting sub-Neptune at the water ice line, offers insights into planetary composition and formation at the critical boundary where volatile elements freeze, potentially illuminating pathways to habitability.

**Atmospheric Dynamics in Tidally Locked Worlds**: New research on atmospheric circulation patterns in periodically irradiated tidally locked planets provides essential insights into how atmospheres behave on exoplanets in synchronous rotation—a common configuration for planets in the habitable zones of red dwarf stars.

## arxiv astro-ph

### Toddlers in Resonance I: Transit Timing Variations and Orbital Parameters of the Young Two-Planet System HIP 67522 bc
- **Authors:** Hannah R. James, Madyson G. Barber, Andrew W. Mann, Pa Chia Thao, Adam L. Kraus, et al.
- **Summary:** Analysis of transit timing variations in the 17 Myr old system HIP 67522 bc reveals planetary masses (~25 and ~11 Earth masses) with low eccentricities, confirming predictions from contracting atmosphere planet formation models. Transit timing variation masses are essential for testing theories of super-Earth and sub-Neptune formation and evolution.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35979

### Atmospheric Dynamics and Variability of Periodically Irradiated Tidally Locked Planets
- **Authors:** Deepayan Banik, Vasilii Pustovoit, Thaddeus D. Komacek, Marta L. Bryan
- **Summary:** Examines how planets locked in synchronous rotation to their host stars experience atmospheric circulation patterns and variability under periodic stellar irradiation. These atmospheric dynamics are crucial for understanding habitability on tidally locked exoplanets, particularly those orbiting in the habitable zones of low-mass stars.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38128

### Improved Constraints on the Surface of LHS 3844 b from its Mid-Infrared Spectrum
- **Authors:** Kimberly Paragas, Heather A. Knutson, Renyu Hu, Bethany L. Ehlmann, Aishwarya R. Iyer, Sebastian Zieba, Laura Kreidberg
- **Summary:** Uses mid-infrared spectroscopic observations to refine understanding of an exoplanet's surface characteristics and composition. Improved constraints on LHS 3844 b's surface properties contribute to our understanding of atmospheric retention and surface conditions on rocky exoplanets.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37982

### TOI-6981 b: A Transiting Sub-Neptune at the Water Ice Line
- **Authors:** Madison G. Scott, Amaury H.M.J Triaud, Emily K. Pass, and 46 co-authors
- **Summary:** Discovery and characterization of a sub-Neptune planet orbiting within the region where water transitions from liquid to ice. This discovery provides critical insights into planetary composition and formation at the water ice line, a boundary potentially relevant to habitability and volatile retention.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37597

### Constraining Exoplanet Population Parameters with Kepler and Simulation-Based Inference
- **Authors:** Chloé Padois, Friedrich Anders, Daniel del Ser
- **Summary:** Applies advanced simulation-based inference methods to characterize the broader population of planets detected by the Kepler mission. Statistical constraints on planetary population parameters help contextualize individual discoveries within the broader demographics of planetary systems.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37960

### The Occurrence Rate of Planets Around Subgiant Stars from TESS Photometric Survey
- **Authors:** Arkaprova Dutta, Priyanka Chaturvedi
- **Summary:** Analyzes the frequency and occurrence rate of planetary systems around evolved subgiant stars using TESS observations. Expanding our understanding of planetary demographics across different stellar evolutionary stages is essential for comprehensive models of planetary system formation and evolution.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37517

## Speculative Corner

The discoveries of sub-Neptunes at the water ice line and detailed atmospheric dynamics models for tidally locked planets paint an intriguing picture of habitability on distant worlds. TOI-6981 b, positioned exactly at the cosmic freezing point where water transforms from liquid to solid, represents a critical juncture in planetary chemistry. On such a world, subsurface hydrothermal systems—heated by residual planetary heat or tidal friction—could maintain liquid water reservoirs beneath a frozen surface, providing potential niches for microbial life even in seemingly frozen conditions.

The new research on tidally locked atmospheres is particularly fascinating for astrobiology. These synchronously rotating worlds experience perpetual day-night cycles in their fixed reference frames, with atmospheric winds driven by the stark thermal gradient between the eternal day and eternal night sides. Recent models suggest these atmospheres might not be as stagnant as once feared; instead, they develop complex circulation patterns that could distribute heat and chemical nutrients more effectively. For extremophile microbes—thermophiles or psychrophiles—this dynamic environment could provide unexpected ecological niches where atmospheric circulation drives nutrient cycling and energy gradients.

The characterization of young planetary systems like HIP 67522 bc, with low-eccentricity orbits and masses in the sub-Neptune range, hints at formation pathways that may favor stable, long-term habitability. These systems formed in the aftermath of the cosmic dust-clearing phase, suggesting that planets formed in less turbulent environments may maintain atmospheres longer and develop more stable chemical cycles—the prerequisites for life's emergence. As we continue to catalog and characterize this diversity of planetary environments, we move closer to identifying worlds where microbial life might actually thrive.
